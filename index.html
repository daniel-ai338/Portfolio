01 Selected work

Three things I shipped, and what they actually took.

Live sites, not case-study fiction. Each one had a different hard part — a legacy stack, a content model, or an AI workflow that had to stay cheap under load.

Case 01

Driven Dispatch Solutions

AI SaaS platform for truck dispatch · full-stack & architecture

A subscription platform that takes the repetitive half of a dispatcher's day — sourcing loads, drafting broker replies, keeping paperwork straight — and hands it to an LLM workflow. Owner-operators sign in to a dashboard where every job, document and automation run is in one place.

My role
Sole developer: system architecture, frontend, backend APIs, database design, AI integration and deployment.
Hard part
Keeping the AI step predictable and cheap — structured outputs, retries and caching so a bad model day doesn't become a bad customer day.
Outcome
A scalable MVP in production, with room to bolt on further AI features and third-party integrations without a rewrite.
  • Next.js
  • React
  • Node.js
  • PostgreSQL
  • LLM API
  • Subscriptions
drivendispatchsolutions.com

Case 02

Silverstein Songs

Music publishing & artist site for a Tony-nominated songwriter

A catalogue site built around listening: songs, co-writers, sessions and news, all reachable in a couple of taps. The brief was editorial — it had to feel like a record sleeve, not a CMS — while staying fast enough that a track starts before attention drifts.

My role
Full-stack engineer — interface, API layer, database modelling, SEO and cross-browser work.
Hard part
A content model flexible enough for songs, writers and sessions to cross-reference each other without the editor needing a manual.
Outcome
A fast, responsive site on a clean codebase the client's next developer can pick up without a handover call.
  • Next.js
  • React
  • TypeScript
  • PostgreSQL
  • Python
silversteinsongs.com

Case 03

Tee Shirt Lady

E-commerce store with a customer slogan competition

An apparel shop with a twist: customers submit slogans, and one gets printed each month. That meant a normal storefront plus a submission and moderation flow, all inside WordPress without turning the admin into a plugin graveyard.

My role
Full-stack developer — requirements, theme customisation, custom PHP and jQuery features, responsive build, deployment.
Hard part
Performance. An inherited theme and a growing catalogue meant trimming queries and assets rather than adding another caching plugin.
Outcome
A production storefront that holds up on desktop, tablet and phone, with a noticeably lighter page than the one it replaced.
  • WordPress
  • PHP
  • jQuery
  • MongoDB
  • Responsive & SEO
teeshirtlady.com

02 About

I learned systems before I learned to like them.

My first job out of university was at a Japanese software house, writing backends for clients who measured quality in uptime. Nearly four years there taught me the unglamorous half of the craft: how a schema you chose in week one decides what you can build in year two, and why the interesting bugs never show up in staging.

From 2024 to early 2026 I worked with a Toronto team on AI products — React and Next.js on the front, Python and FastAPI behind it, with Claude and LangChain doing the work that used to need a person and a spreadsheet. That combination is where I'm most useful now: close enough to the model to know what it will get wrong, and close enough to the database to make sure it doesn't matter.

I like problems that arrive slightly broken — the platform that grew faster than its architecture, the prototype that needs to become a product. Fixing those is usually cheaper than the rebuild everyone is bracing for.

  • Constraints first, framework second

    Budget, team size and deadline decide the stack more honestly than taste does.

  • Something real in week one

    A thin slice you can click beats a document describing a slice you can't.

  • Boring where it counts

    Auth, payments and data go with the proven option. Save the novelty for the product.

  • Written to be handed over

    If the next developer needs me on a call to understand it, I haven't finished.
